# Engine repair + edge search — design

**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Status:** approved design, not yet planned
**Supersedes nothing.** Continues `.planning/` Phase 4 (measurement) and consumes the
verdicts in `.planning/evidence/killtests/`.

---

## 1. The question this answers

The operator asked why paper trading produces losing trades, and for the system to be
made profitable.

The first half is answerable and answered below. The second half is not a plannable
deliverable and this document does not pretend otherwise: no strategy in this repo has
demonstrated edge (CLAUDE.md §2), and LIVE trading at $100 is mechanically impossible
regardless of edge (the 2% LIVE cap is $2, below the ~$5 venue minimum notional). What
*is* deliverable is a cost hurdle any candidate must clear, a loop that kills candidates
cheaply against it, and an engine that measures honestly enough to trust the answer.

## 2. Evidence base

All figures are from the live system or committed evidence files, not from documentation.

### 2.1 The signal has no directional information

`.planning/evidence/killtests/H4-verdict-20260806.md` — **REJECT**. The real deployed
stack (TA service + `SignalAggregator` + `MultiStrategyEnsemble`) replayed over 365 days
of gap-free mainnet candles across all 5 symbols:

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| directional accuracy | 49.07% (n = 12,387 signals) |
| DSR | 0.0 |
| pooled profit factor | 1.013 (fee-free) |

Per that verdict's own caveat, 49.07% must **not** be read as an invertible contrarian
signal — with ~24-fold window overlap the effective sample is ~n/24, whose standard error
covers 50%. The correct reading is "indistinguishable from chance".

### 2.2 The economics, measured

`.planning/evidence/killtests/H3-secondary-verdict-20260806.md`, n = 12,297 replayed
trades generated by the ensemble's own fired signals:

| | 1.5× ATR | 2.5× ATR |
|---|---|---|
| gross edge / trade, % of notional | **0.0488%** | 0.0128% |
| round-trip cost, Bybit taker est. | 0.110% | 0.110% |
| net total | **−$52.49** | −$83.37 |
| funding, not included in the above | ~$48.6 | ~$50.8 |

Costs exceed gross edge by 2.3×–4.1× at 1.5× ATR and 8.6×–15.6× at 2.5×. There is no fee
tier or execution improvement of that magnitude available to a $100 account.

**This is the answer to "why am I getting losing trades."** The signal earns roughly 5
basis points per trade and it costs 21–31 basis points in fees and slippage to take,
before signed funding.

19 positions, 16 closed, net **+$0.10** — flat, not down. 11 losses / 5 wins.

Four of the five wins came from a single flatten-and-reopen event at 2026-08-04 14:10:
five positions closed between 14:10:09 and 14:10:20, four reopened between 14:10:25 and

The live sample therefore supports no conclusion about exit design, and its winning side

### 2.6 How the thresholds got here

Git history establishes the pattern that produced a chance-level signal. Commit `1295865`
(2025-11-26), *"enable aggressive paper trading mode for immediate trade execution"*,
states the bot had not traded for a month and lowered `min_confidence` 0.60 → **0.01**
("essentially no minimum"), flipping `auto_trading` to `true` in the same commit. Its
stated success criterion is *"Bot executed first trade immediately after changes"* — a
trade happening, not a trade making money.

`56ed13c` (2026-05-06) short-circuits `_passes_min_notional` in PAPER mode explicitly to
avoid trade count going to zero — a direct violation of CLAUDE.md §1 ("a trade below
min-notional must be rejected with a reason"). `aaa193a` (2026-05-08) increased position
size while stating on the record: *"the live ensemble has no proven edge … Sizing up an
unedged strategy bleeds faster."*

Separately, several in-code comments documenting threshold changes describe values that
`git log -S` proves **never existed in any commit** (`aggregator_core.py:105-106`,
`gatekeeper.py:86`, `:99`). Every divergence understates the loosening. Those comments
are not usable as history.

## 2.7 Scope of the first implementation plan

This document describes three stages. Only **Stage 0 (§4) plus L1 and L2 of Stage 1
(§5.2, §5.3)** is a single implementation plan. L3, L4, the candidate work, and Stage 2
each get their own plan once the preceding one has landed and been verified. Stage 2 is
deliberately unplanned until a candidate survives L3, because its priority order depends
on which candidate that is.

## 3. Non-goals

- Promising profitability. Nothing in this design guarantees a positive-expectancy
  strategy will be found. It guarantees that a losing one will be identified quickly and
  cheaply, and that a winning one could be recognized if it appeared.
- Enabling LIVE trading. The 2% LIVE cap is $2 against a ~$5 venue minimum. Unchanged and
  unchangeable by code.
- Adding indicators. Adding a sixth indicator to five losing indicators produces a losing
  ensemble.
- Re-litigating AUDIT.md or the Phase 1 fixes, which are committed and verified.

## 4. Stage 0 — stop the bleeding

Small, live-critical, and deliberately free of anything that changes strategy behavior.
Ships before any measurement is trusted.

### S0-1 — leverage becomes an immutable property of a position

Fixes E1 and E2.

- Migration `008_position_leverage.sql`: add `positions.leverage NUMERIC(10,4) NOT NULL DEFAULT 1.0`.
- Stamp it on open from `settings.default_leverage`; read it from the row at close,
  reduce, and restart reconstruction. Three sites: `paper_trading.py:253`, `:93-107`, `:447`.
- Backfill existing rows from the actual cutoff, established by reading the commit that
  changed `DEFAULT_LEVERAGE` in `docker-compose.unified.yml` (`a51e815`) together with the
  container's recreation time — not assumed from the commit date alone. Positions opened
  before that instant get `10.0`; after, `1.0`.
- One-time ledger repair: `portfolios.cash_balance = initial_balance + Σ realized_pnl(closed) − Σ margin(open)`.
- Standing invariant, asserted in a test that runs on every close path:
  `cash_balance + Σ open margin + Σ unrealized == initial_balance + Σ realized_pnl`.

**Acceptance:** the invariant query returns true; `get_balance()`,
`portfolios.cash_balance`, and `/api/v1/performance` agree to the cent; a simulated
`DEFAULT_LEVERAGE` change with an open position does not move the ledger.

### S0-2 — persist the true exit reason

Fixes E3.

- Introduce `ExitKind` enum: `HARD_STOP | TRAILING_STOP | TP1 | TP2 | TP3 | MAX_HOLD | MANUAL | SIGNAL_REVERSAL`.
- Carry it from the decision site through `OrderCreate` to `positions.exit_reason` as a
  dedicated column, rather than reconstructing a prose string at `paper_trading.py:343-345`.
- Retire the substring routing at `auto_trader.py:2883-2888` as the taxonomy source; it
  may remain as the *routing* mechanism, but must not be the record.

**Acceptance:** for each of the eight `ExitKind` values, a test drives the engine to that
exit and asserts the persisted value. Without this, nothing else in Stage 0 or Stage 2 is
verifiable.

### S0-3 — the ensemble path enforces its gates

Fixes E4 and E5.

- Attach the strategy's own `stop_loss` / `take_profit` to `OrderCreate` at

**Acceptance:** an entry at confidence 0.2357 is rejected with a logged reason; a

## 5. Stage 1 — the research loop

Organized around one number.

### 5.1 The cost hurdle

```
fees + slippage, taker, majors (BTC/ETH/SOL)  2 × 5.5bp + 2 × 5bp   = 21 bp
fees + slippage, taker, BNB/ADA               2 × 5.5bp + 2 × 10bp  = 31 bp
fees, maker-only (PostOnly, both legs)        2 × 2bp               =  4 bp
```

Fee and slippage values from `config.py:576` (`paper_commission_pct = 0.055`) and
`paper_slippage.py:92-98`.

**Funding is not a flat adder and must not be modeled as one.** It is *signed*,
per-symbol, and per-side: a cost only when holding the side that pays. Measured live
2026-08-07 across 200 settlements per symbol, the mean per 8h is 0.00278% (BTC), 0.00191%
(ETH), **−0.00086% (SOL)**, 0.00319% (BNB), 0.00137% (ADA) — so over a 48h hold the
typical magnitude is **1–2 bp**, not 6, and a long in SOL is *paid*. The 0.0100% figure
seen on all five symbols is the base-rate clip, i.e. the worst case (~6 bp over 48h), not
the norm. `costs.py` computes funding from the backfilled per-symbol series against the
actual side and holding period; it never appears as a constant in the hurdle table.

**Hurdle: gross edge per trade ≥ 2× the applicable all-in cost.**

| execution | fees + slippage | hurdle | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| taker | 21–31 bp | **≥ ~0.45–0.65%** | plus signed funding |
| maker-only | 4 bp | **≥ ~0.10%** | only if the fill model survives a realism check |

The current ensemble delivers **0.049%** — below even the maker-only hurdle.

Execution mode is not a constraint on candidates. Every candidate is scored under both
assumptions and the hurdle decides. The maker-only column is *not* a free 5× reduction:
its 4 bp covers explicit fees only, and adverse selection and non-fill are real costs that
`costs.py` does not capture as a spread. A candidate that clears only under maker-only
assumptions must additionally demonstrate a fill model that accounts for the orders that
never fill — otherwise its hurdle reverts to the taker column.

### 5.2 L1 — `costs.py`, one cost model

A single module owning: Bybit fee tier with maker and taker separated, per-symbol
slippage, tick and lot-step quantization, min-notional **rejection** (never clamp up —
CLAUDE.md §1), and funding accrual per settlement.

Imported by the live paper engine *and* every backtester, replacing the divergent
implementations catalogued in M4.

**Acceptance (parity test), and its deliberate limit:** the same trade — entry, partial
exit, stop exit, full exit — routed through the live paper engine and through the offline
simulator must agree to the cent on the **deterministic** components: fees, per-symbol
slippage, tick and lot quantization, min-notional rejection, and signed funding.

**Stop-fill modeling is explicitly excluded from parity until E9 lands.** The live engine
is optimistic by construction — `auto_trader.py:3311-3313` passes the stop price as the
fill reference regardless of how far the 1h bar closed through it, so a bar that gaps 4%
through a 2% stop still books exactly −2%. Requiring parity there would mean building the
simulator to reproduce that optimism, and the screen in §5.3 would inherit an unknown
optimistic bias on precisely the trades that decide a verdict. The simulator therefore
uses the **pessimistic** rule — fill at bar close when the bar closes through the stop,
matching the stop-first intrabar convention `BacktestEngine` and
`backtesting/killtests/h3_atr_replay.py` already implement. Parity on this component is
re-enabled only after E9 corrects the live side, and the divergence is asserted as
*expected* in the meantime rather than silently tolerated.

### 5.3 L2 — `screen.py`, the hurdle-first killer

Input: a signal series (`timestamp, symbol, side`).
Output: gross edge per trade, all-in cost per trade under both execution modes, the ratio,
and PASS/KILL — in seconds.

Runs *before* any statistics. Most candidates die here, which is the point: CLAUDE.md's
stated value of this infrastructure is killing bad strategies cheaply.

### 5.4 L3 — `verdict.py`, the statistics that do not currently exist

Composes the missing path identified in M1:

1. fee-inclusive strategy **P&L series** (not signed returns), produced via `costs.py`
2. purged and embargoed CPCV (`cpcv.py` exists and works)
3. DSR with M2 fixed — correct z-scaling and the skew/kurtosis denominator restored
4. **PBO** (probability of backtest overfitting), newly implemented
5. `num_trials` sourced from a project-wide ledger file, not from the length of one run's
   path list

**Gate — all four, or KILL:**
`DSR > 0.95` **AND** `PBO < 0.5` **AND** net-of-cost expectancy > 0 **AND** n ≥ 200 trades.

### 5.5 L4 — pre-registration

Every candidate gets a hypothesis file committed *before* it is run, stating: the claimed
inefficiency, why it should persist, expected holding period, expected gross edge as a
percentage of notional, and the exact parameter grid to be searched.

`num_trials` accumulates across the whole project and feeds L3. This is the direct
antidote to §2.6.

## 6. Stage 2 — engine repair on demand

Executed only when a candidate survives L3, and prioritized by what that candidate needs.
Ordered by measurement impact:

1. E10 — funding accrual in the paper engine
2. E9 — price source from 1h bar close to tick or 1m, and honest stop fills
3. E6 — exit legs must snap to step and respect min-notional
4. E7 + E8 — trailing stop self-disable, and trailing fills booked at the ratcheted level
5. E11 — TP ladder geometry, and a ladder that can skip levels in one cycle
6. E12 — partial-exit P&L reported net
7. E13 — `PerformanceMetrics` actually computing PF, avg win/loss, drawdown
8. E14 — BNB tick size from the venue, not a literal
9. M3 — walk-forward runner pointed at the strategy actually in production
10. M6 — `tests/killtests/` into CI

## 7. Verification standard

CLAUDE.md §7 applies to every claim in Stage 0 and Stage 2. An HTTP 200 is not proof. A
change is done when: the invariant or acceptance test passes, a DB row is pasted showing
the new behavior, and the service whose config changed was recreated (not restarted).

No edge claim is accepted without L3's four-part gate. No backtest figure predating
2026-08-03 is citable without re-running it (CLAUDE.md §2).

## 8. Risks

- **The search finds nothing.** The most likely outcome. The loop is designed so that
  outcome arrives in days rather than months, and so that it is unambiguous when it does.
- **A false positive survives L3.** M2's DSR bug currently makes a false positive
  *impossible* only because the statistic is a step function pinned at zero by negative
  Sharpes; once fixed, a strategy with tight fold agreement and a trivially positive mean
  could clear it. PBO and the project-wide `num_trials` ledger exist specifically to
  cover that gap.
- **The parity test passes while both engines share a wrong assumption.** Mitigated by
  validating `costs.py` against live venue specs from the connector, not against either
  engine's existing behavior.
- **Stage 0's ledger repair is destructive to a DB row.** It rewrites
  `portfolios.cash_balance`. Take a `pg_dump` of `positions`, `trades`, and `portfolios`
  first; the repair must be expressible as a reversible SQL script with a recorded
  before-value.
